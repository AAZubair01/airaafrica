# AIRA Africa — Founding Playbook (No Sugar Coating)

**Audience:** Founders and Board only. Not a public marketing page.  
**Reality check:** You are CAC-registered. That is necessary and almost worthless by itself. Funders do not fund RC numbers. They fund proof of work, governance, and financial controls.

---

## 1. The hard truth

1. **No office and no computer is normal at day zero — and fatal if it stays that way.** You do not need a rented suite. You do need: one reliable laptop, phone data you can pay for, cloud storage, and a corporate bank account.
2. **Five focus areas is too many.** Pick health + agriculture for the next 18–24 months. Everything else is a brochure fantasy until you have mentors and money.
3. **Grants do not fund “ideas.”** They fund organisations that can receive money legally, spend it cleanly, report on it, and show prior activity (even small).
4. **Volunteer enthusiasm dies without jobs.** Publish specific roles, hours, and deliverables — which the website now does. Follow up within 72 hours or people leave.
5. **Gmail as the official inbox looks amateur to international funders.** Move to `info@airaafrica.com` (or similar) as soon as DNS/mail is set up. Keep the Gmail as a temporary bridge, not a permanent brand.

---

## 2. First 90 days (in order)

Do these in sequence. Skipping ahead to “write big proposals” wastes time.

### Week 1–2 — Make the organisation bankable

- [ ] Open a **corporate bank account** in the Ltd/GTE name (bring CAC docs, board resolution, ID of signatories).
- [ ] Decide **two signatories**; never one person alone for large withdrawals.
- [ ] Create a shared drive (Google Workspace / Microsoft 365 nonprofit rates when eligible) for: CAC docs, constitution/MEMART, minutes, receipts.
- [ ] Buy or allocate **one dedicated laptop** for Institute work (even if personally funded and later reimbursed with a paper trail).
- [ ] Set up **professional email** on airaafrica.com.
- [ ] Replace placeholder team photo for the Company Secretary.

### Week 3–6 — Governance that donors check

- [ ] Write a one-page **Board charter**: who decides what, meeting cadence, conflict-of-interest policy.
- [ ] Recruit **at least one independent Board member** with a reputation you do not control (university, civil society, finance).
- [ ] Adopt a simple **finance policy**: petty cash limit, receipt rule, monthly reconciliation.
- [ ] Draft a **volunteer agreement** (role, confidentiality, unpaid status, IP for curricula).

### Week 6–12 — Proof of work (this unlocks grants)

- [ ] Recruit **5–10 mentors** and **10–20 mentee applicants**.
- [ ] Run a **pilot mentorship cycle** (8–12 weeks, remote, documented).
- [ ] Keep: attendance log, pre/post self-assessment, 3 short case stories, one public update.
- [ ] Publish a **2–4 page pilot report** as PDF on the website.
- [ ] Host **one free public webinar** (research methods or grant-writing basics) and record attendance.

**Without that pilot report, most serious funders will ignore you.** Local seed money and in-kind support are more realistic first.

## 3. Volunteers — how to recruit without wasting people

### Who to target

| Role | Where to find them | What to offer |
|------|--------------------|---------------|
| Research mentors | BUK, ABU, sister universities; LinkedIn; professional associations (radiography, agriculture) | Clear time bound (e.g. 4 hrs/month), certificate, co-branded profile |
| Programme coordinator | Recent graduates, NYSC alumni who are organisers | Real responsibility, reference letter, title on LinkedIn |
| Finance volunteer | Accountants in Kano diaspora WhatsApp groups; ICAN students | Portfolio experience; letter of service |
| Communications | Journalism/comms students; diaspora writers | Portfolio pieces; honest storytelling brief |
| Mentees | Department WhatsApp groups, notice boards, HODs | Structured mentoring, not empty “membership” |

### Rules that keep volunteers

1. **One owner per role.** Ambiguous “everyone helps” means nobody does.
2. **Weekly 30-minute standup** (WhatsApp voice or Zoom). Cancel if nothing to discuss — but never ghost people.
3. **Public credit.** Name volunteers on the Updates page when they ship something.
4. **Fire kindly.** If someone disappears twice, replace them. Dead weight kills culture.

## 4. Visibility — what actually works at your stage

Stop fantasising about “going viral.” Do boring, targeted visibility.

1. **LinkedIn company page + founder posts** — weekly: one lesson from building AIRA, one call for mentors, one legal/status update. No stock-photo “impact.”
2. **WhatsApp broadcast / community** — one list for mentors, one for mentee interest. Consent-based.
3. **University liaison** — visit 2–3 HODs in Kano with a one-pager (mission, pilot design, ask). Face-to-face still beats cold email in Nigeria.
4. **Partner for borrowed trust** — ask an established lab, faculty, or NGO to co-host the first webinar. Their audience is your oxygen.
5. **Photos of real work** — once the pilot starts, replace every logo-only image on the site with real sessions (with consent).

Avoid: buying fake followers; claiming partners you do not have; posting AI-generated “event photos.”

## 5. Grants and startup funding — blunt map

### What you can get now (realistic)

| Source | Typical size | Fit for AIRA now | Notes |
|--------|--------------|------------------|-------|
| Founders + friends/family gifts | Small | Yes | Document as donations; issue receipts |
| Local philanthropists / business owners in Kano | ₦100k–few million | Yes if relationship exists | Ask for **equipment + data + pilot costs**, not “institutional support” |
| In-kind (venue, Zoom, printing, airtime) | Non-cash | Yes | Treat as sponsorship; acknowledge publicly |
| University collaboration (no cash) | Staff time | Yes | MoU for mentorship hosting |
| Nigerian small grants / challenge funds | Varies | Maybe | Often need 6–12 months activity + audited accounts |
| International foundation grants | Large | **Not yet** | Usually need audited financials, track record, sometimes foreign registration or fiscal sponsor |
| Crowdfunding | Unpredictable | Possible later | Needs story + proof + payment rails |

### What funders will ask (prepare folders)

1. CAC certificate + status report  
2. MEMART / constitution showing non-profit objects  
3. Board list and CVs  
4. Bank account details + recent statements  
5. Simple budget and work plan  
6. Safeguarding / conflict of interest policy (even a short one)  
7. Evidence of past activity (pilot report)  
8. Tax / SCUML / any sector registration if required for your activities  

### How to approach grants without embarrassing yourself

1. **Budget a real pilot** (example line items): laptop, internet, Zoom/pro tools, facilitator stipends (even small), data for mentors, design of curriculum PDF, basic accounting software.
2. **Ask for 3–6 months of operating costs for one programme**, not a five-year empire.
3. **Use a fiscal sponsor** if a funder will not pay a brand-new Nigerian Ltd/GTE yet (partner with a mature NGO that receives the grant and sub-grants to you under an MoU). Pride is expensive.
4. **Never invent beneficiaries.** One documented cohort of 15 beats “we will reach 10,000 youth.”
5. **Read eligibility twice.** Many Africa research grants require existing IRBs, prior publications, or institutional affiliation with a university. Partner accordingly.

### Places to research (verify current calls yourself)

- TETFUND-related collaboration via university partners (you are not a university; ride with one)  
- State / federal youth or research innovation calls (eligibility varies wildly)  
- Regional research capacity programmes (e.g. partnerships with AAS, university networks — usually competitive and track-record heavy)  
- Corporate CSR in health and agribusiness in Northern Nigeria (relationship-driven)  
- Diaspora giving circles  

Treat every online “guaranteed grant” broker as a scam until proven otherwise.

## 6. Activities that make a nonprofit “real”

Minimum viable activity stack for the next year:

1. **Mentorship pilot** (core)  
2. **Monthly methods webinar** (visibility + value)  
3. **One partnership MoU** with a department or NGO  
4. **Quarterly Board meeting with minutes**  
5. **Public annual note** (even 3 pages): money in, money out, what we did, what failed  

That is enough to stop looking like a name on a certificate.

---

## 7. What success looks like in 12 months (honest targets)

- Corporate account + professional email live  
- 8+ active mentors; 1 completed mentee cohort with report  
- 1 institutional MoU  
- Basic bookkeeping and Board minutes for the year  
- Website showing real photos and named partners (only real ones)  
- First external funding — even if small or in-kind — tied to the pilot  

If you hit that, you are a young but serious institute.  
If you only have a prettier website and five empty programme pages, you are still a brochure.

---

## 8. Founder discipline

- Work AIRA like a second job with a calendar, or it will die under clinical/PhD load.  
- Separate personal WhatsApp drama from Institute channels.  
- Say **no** to every new “focus area,” conference, or MOU theatre until the mentorship pilot ships.  
- Measure weekly: mentors recruited, mentee applications, tasks closed — not likes.

Registration was the easy part. Delivery is the job.
